The EU stay checker says “Not OK”? The 6 usual causes, and what to do

The EU's Check how long you can stay service only knows what the Entry/Exit System (EES) recorded. When a crossing is missing or outside its data, the result can be wrong in either direction. Find your situation below.

1. Your exit was not recorded

The most reported problem. When queues get long, some borders temporarily suspend EES and check passports by hand, and travellers have reported being waved through without any exit record. The system then believes you are still in the Schengen area and keeps counting, sometimes until it shows zero days left while you are at home. It does not correct itself: once your authorised stay runs out, a record without an exit is flagged as a possible overstay.

What to do: collect proof of the exit (boarding pass, ferry or tunnel booking, a receipt from outside the Schengen area that day), take a dated screenshot of the checker, and ask the border authority to correct the record under Article 52 of the EES Regulation. Our free correction kit writes the request for you. Travellers have also had officers add a note or enter a missing exit manually at their next crossing when they showed evidence, so carry it with you.

2. “Cannot currently be calculated”

Some travellers get a message that their remaining stay cannot currently be calculated due to temporary incomplete data instead of a number. This happens when the records do not pair up: an entry with no exit, or an exit with no entry. It is not a decision against you, but it signals a gap that may cause trouble at your next border.

What to do: work out which crossing is missing (your own trip log helps), then request access to your EES record and its completion. Asking for access first is useful if you are not sure which crossing is wrong: the authority must tell you what is recorded.

3. A crossing has the wrong date

Less common, but a wrong date shifts the whole 180-day window. Compare the checker's result with your own count. If they differ by a few days and you have no pre-April 2026 trips, a date is probably wrong.

What to do: same route: evidence of the real date, then a rectification request.

4. Days before 10 April 2026 are ignored

EES became fully operational on 10 April 2026, and the checker only uses EES data. A trip in March 2026 is invisible to it, yet still counts under the 90/180 rule until it leaves the 180-day window. This is not an error in your record, but it means the checker can say “OK” when you would in fact be over the limit. Border officers can still count those days, for example from stamps.

What to do: count everything yourself with a calculator that includes pre-EES trips, such as ours, and plan on the stricter result.

5. An entry is missing

Kiosk failures, manual lanes and repeated fingerprint problems have all been reported. A missing entry usually shows up as incomplete data later, when your exit has no matching entry.

What to do: keep the evidence of your arrival and request completion of the record, especially before your next trip.

6. You should not be in the count at all

People who hold a residence permit or long-stay visa of a Schengen country, and UK nationals with a Withdrawal Agreement residence card, are not subject to the 90/180 limit in the country concerned. If the system treats you as a short-stay visitor, show your document at the border and ask for the record to be corrected.

If you have already been stopped

A refusal of entry, a removal order or a re-entry ban are serious decisions with short deadlines to challenge them, sometimes only a few days. In France, for example, a removal order (OQTF) can come with a ban on returning to the whole Schengen area. Photograph the decision, note the date and the deadline written on it, and contact a lawyer immediately. Correcting your EES record is still worth doing in parallel.

Prevent it next time

  • Keep boarding passes and bookings for at least six months.
  • Check the official checker and your own log before each trip, and plan with a margin of a few days.
  • If your exit is not scanned, ask the officer to record it, or keep proof of that day.
