How the 90/180-day rule is counted

Non-EU visitors may stay in the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. The 180 days are not a calendar half-year: they roll. On any given day, look back at that day and the 179 days before it, and count every day you were in the Schengen area. If the total is above 90, you are over the limit.

  • Entry and exit days both count, even a day trip.
  • All Schengen countries share one counter: days in France and days in Spain add up.
  • Days spent under a national long-stay visa or residence permit of a Schengen country do not count towards the short-stay limit in that country. If that applies to you, leave those periods out.
  • Ireland and Cyprus are in the EU but not in the Schengen area for this counting.

What changed with the Entry/Exit System (EES)

EES started in October 2025 and has been fully operational at all Schengen external borders since 10 April 2026. Your crossings are now recorded digitally with your passport, face and fingerprints, and passports are no longer stamped. Two practical consequences:

  1. You lose your own paper record. Stamps used to let you count your days. Now the record sits in a database you cannot browse, so keeping your own log is the simplest protection.
  2. The official checker can be incomplete. The EU's Check how long you can stay service only uses EES data. Days before 10 April 2026 are not included, and a crossing that was not recorded properly (for example an exit during a busy period when stamps were used instead) can make it show the wrong result.

Trips that started before 10 April 2026 are flagged pre-EES in your list: they still count, but the official checker does not see them.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the official checker show more days left than this calculator?

Usually because some of your days are from before 10 April 2026. They still count for the rule while they are within the last 180 days, but EES never recorded them. Border officers can still take them into account, for example from stamps in your passport.

The official checker says I have overstayed, but I haven't

That usually means an exit was not recorded or a date is wrong. It does not correct itself. Gather your evidence and ask for your record to be checked and corrected: our EES correction kit walks you through it and writes the request for you.

Does a transit through a Schengen airport count?

Staying airside in the international transit area does not count as an entry. Passing through passport control does, and that day counts.

Is this legal advice?

No. This is a free calculator that applies the published counting rule to the dates you enter. Border authorities make the final decision. If you are close to the limit or have an unusual situation, check with the authorities or a lawyer.
