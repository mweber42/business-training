The new EU flight compensation rules: what changes, and from when

The reform of EU261 is now law: Regulation (EU) 2026/2202 entered into force on 22 October 2026. But it only applies from 23 October 2027. Until then, nothing changes for your claims.

What stays the same

  • Delay threshold: compensation when you reach your final destination more than three hours late. Proposals to raise it to 4 or 6 hours were dropped.
  • Amounts: €250 up to 1,500 km; €400 for intra-EU journeys over 1,500 km and other journeys of 1,500 to 3,500 km; €600 beyond.
  • Extraordinary circumstances still exempt the airline, which must prove them.

What changes from 23 October 2027

  • Distance is measured per journey, to your final destination, rather than flight by flight.
  • Long-haul reduction: for the €600 band, the airline may halve compensation if you arrive no more than four hours late, after a delay or a later rerouting.
  • Claim deadline and answer: you have nine months from the departure date to claim. The airline must acknowledge your request immediately and, within 30 calendar days, pay or justify a refusal. A third party claiming for you may be asked for proof of mandate.
  • Rerouting: if the airline does not offer comparable rerouting within three hours after a cancellation, you may arrange your own and claim the cost back, up to 400% of the ticket price (plus intermediation fees), to be reimbursed within 14 days.
  • Refunds: legal commentators report a seven-day deadline that includes intermediary fees when you booked through an agency.
  • Extraordinary circumstances are listed in an annex (non-exhaustive), codifying Court of Justice case law.
  • Tarmac delays: regular, real-time updates and a right to disembark, which can only be limited for safety, security, immigration or air traffic control reasons.
  • A voluntary “Union Air Passenger Rights Label” that airlines and booking sites can display. Commentators also report new rules on cabin baggage and on “no-show” cancellations of return flights.

What to do now

  1. Had a delayed or cancelled flight in the last few years? It falls under the current rules, often with a long time limit. Check it and claim it yourself.
  2. From October 2027, claim within nine months. Keep boarding passes and booking emails at least that long.
