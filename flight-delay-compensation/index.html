Flight delayed, cancelled or overbooked? Check what EU261 owes you, then claim it yourself

European rules give up to €600 per passenger. Claims companies keep a large share of it; the airline has to pay you directly if you ask. Check your case in one minute and get a ready-to-send claim letter.

1. Your flight

With connections on one booking, use your final destination.

For flights arriving in Europe from outside, EU261 only applies to European airlines.

Arrival time = when the aircraft doors opened.

2. Your claim letter

Send it to the airline that operated the flight (its online claim form or customer relations email). Your details are not saved or sent anywhere.

  • Which flights: every flight departing from an airport in the EU, Iceland, Norway or Switzerland, and flights arriving there from elsewhere if the airline is European.
    • 
      
  • Delay: 3 hours or more at your final destination (EU Court of Justice, Sturgeon, C-402/07). For flights over 3,500 km outside the EU, a delay of 3 to 4 hours gives half (€300).
    • 
      
  • Cancellation: compensation if you were told less than 14 days before, unless the airline rerouted you within strict time limits.
    • 
      
  • Extraordinary circumstances (bad weather, air traffic control decisions, security risks) exempt the airline. A technical problem normally does not, and neither does a strike by the airline's own staff.
    • 
      
  • Care (meals, drinks, hotel if needed) is due whatever the cause. Keep your receipts.
    • 
    


    
The new 2026 regulation: what changes, and when

    
Regulation (EU) 2026/2202, which reforms EU261, was published on 2 October 2026 and entered into force on 22 October 2026, but most of its rules apply only from 23 October 2027. Until then your claim is decided under the current rules above. From that date, compensation amounts stay at €250, €400 and €600, passengers will have nine months to claim and airlines 30 days to answer. All the changes explained.


    
How long do you have to claim?

    
Under the current rules, the time limit is set by the law of the country where you bring the claim: for example 5 years in France and Spain, 3 years in Germany (from the end of the year of the flight), and 6 years in England and Wales under UK rules. Claim as soon as you can: evidence is easier to gather.
