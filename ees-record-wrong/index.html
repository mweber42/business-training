EES says you overstayed, or an exit wasn't recorded? Here's how to fix it

Also in Français · Español

The Entry/Exit System sometimes misses a crossing or records the wrong date. A missing exit does not fix itself: it ends up flagged as a possible overstay. You have a legal right to see your record and have it corrected. This kit writes the request for you, in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch or Portuguese, addressed to the right authority.

Travelling soon? Carry your evidence (tickets, boarding passes, bookings) and a printed copy of your request. At the border, state the disputed crossing (date, place, direction) and ask the officer to check the EES record itself. If you are refused entry, ask for the decision in writing.

Not sure what is wrong? Read the 6 usual reasons the EU checker says “Not OK”.

The 5 steps

  1. Pin down the wrong crossing. Date, border point, direction, and what should have been recorded. Screenshot the result of the EU's Check how long you can stay service, with the date visible.
  2. Gather evidence showing where you really were: boarding passes, tickets, ferry or tunnel bookings, hotel or car-hire confirmations, card payments outside the Schengen area.
  3. Choose the authority. You may apply to any participating country, but the country whose border made or missed the record is usually the right one. The generator below shows its address and email, taken from the official EES data-protection contact list.
  4. Send your request under Article 52 of Regulation (EU) 2017/2226: access to your data and rectification of the wrong record. Generate it below.
  5. Follow up. Keep proof of sending. No answer after 45 days? Chase in writing, then complain to that country's data protection authority.

Your rights and the time limits

    
Article 52 of the EES Regulation (EU) 2017/2226 gives you the right to access the data recorded about you and to have inaccurate data rectified or completed, and unlawfully recorded data erased. You can apply to any participating country.

    
    
      
  • If the country you wrote to cannot check the data on its own, it must contact the responsible country, which then checks the accuracy of the data.
    • 
      
  • Authorities report that replies can take up to 45 days, and that some corrections cannot be made immediately while parts of the system are still being rolled out.
    • 
      
  • If you get no answer or disagree with the reply, you can complain to the national data protection authority of that country, and ultimately go to its courts.
    • 
    

    
Sources: Regulation (EU) 2017/2226, Article 52; Royal Netherlands Marechaussee guidance on incorrect or missing EES registrations; Which? (15 October 2026) on the official stay checker's limits. Rules last checked 22 October 2026.


    
Common causes of a wrong record

    
    
      
  • Busy periods: some countries temporarily suspended EES checks and stamped passports instead, so crossings from those days may be missing.
    • 
      
  • Unrecorded crossings: a skipped fingerprint scan does not by itself mean a crossing was missed, but crossings can go unrecorded. Only the authority's check of the record itself is conclusive.
    • 
      
  • Pre-EES trips: the checker ignores days before 10 April 2026. That is not an error in your record, but it means the checker's count differs from the real rule. Use our 90/180 calculator to count everything.
    • 
    


    
Questions

    
Does a pending request let me stay longer?
No. A request does not extend your authorised stay. Plan on the basis of your real days and carry evidence when you travel.

    
Should I send my passport?
Never send the original. A copy of the identity page is normally enough. Send only what the authority asks for.

    
Is this legal advice?
No. This is a free tool that helps you exercise a right anyone has. If you have been refused entry, received a removal order or a fine, contact a lawyer quickly: deadlines to challenge those decisions can be short.
